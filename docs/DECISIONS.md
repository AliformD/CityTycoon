# Decisions

A running list of rulings and design decisions, so nobody has to rediscover them. Newest at the bottom of each section. "Angel" means Angel decided it; "Builder" means the builder picked the simplest reading and flagged it for Angel to confirm or change.

## Phase 1: core game loop (2026-10-20)

| # | Decision | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | A player whose cash is below an auction's minimum bid passes automatically and isn't asked. | Builder | Asking a question with only one possible answer slows hot-seat games down. Revisit in Phase 3, when loans let players bid more than their cash. |
| 12 | Auctions of a bankrupt player's properties break ties starting from the player whose turn it is (there is no "lander"). | Builder | Section 8.2 only defines tie order for a lander. |
| 13 | Paying bail takes you back to the normal "choose how to move" step, so "Pay bail" and "Roll" are two separate clicks. | Builder | Matches Section 14.3 ("pay, then roll and move normally") and leaves room for the other move methods later. |

| 15 | Railroad and utility rent counts mortgaged railroads and utilities the owner holds. | Builder | Section 13.1 only says pending ones don't count. |
| 16 | The Sim stops a Classic game after 500 rounds and reports it as "stalled" instead of an error. With no buildings until Phase 3, most Classic games stall, which is expected for now. | Builder | Classic mode has no round limit, so a safety stop is needed. |

## Phase 3: building and money (2026-10-20)

| # | Decision | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 21 | Only the player whose turn it is can offer a trade, and only at the "choose how to move" or "anything else before you end your turn?" moments (or in Jail). | Builder | Section 15.9 says "any player at any time the game is waiting on a normal turn action". With one shared screen, letting every player interrupt at once gets confusing. Easy to widen later. |
| 22 | No counter-offers: the other player accepts or declines. To change a deal, decline and offer again. | Builder | Keeps the first version simple. |
| 23 | A player can offer at most 3 trades per turn (`maxTradeProposalsPerTurn` in `rules.json`). | Builder | Stops a player (or a bot) flooding someone with offers. |
| 24 | A trade is checked again at the moment it's accepted. If something changed (for example the cash is gone), it's cancelled with a reason in the log. | Builder | The world can change between offer and answer. |
| 25 | Selling a building gives back half of its full cost before any discounts. | Builder | Section 9.3 says "half of what that level cost". Discounts arrive with characters and tiles, and this stops "build cheap, sell high" tricks. |

| 27 | When someone goes bankrupt, their pending properties go back to the bank and are auctioned, even if they owed another player. | Builder | A pending property was never fully paid for, so it isn't theirs to hand over. |
| 28 | Replaces #11: a player passes an auction automatically only if they couldn't reach the minimum bid **even after taking every loan they can and using a down payment**. | Builder | Loans and down payments now let people bid above their cash. |
| 29 | Replaces #14: owing more cash than you have now opens a "raise money" choice (mortgage, sell buildings, take loans). You go bankrupt only when even all of that wouldn't be enough. | Builder | Section 15.10. |
| 30 | During an auction, the only "manage money" action allowed is taking a loan. | Builder | Section 8.4. |
| 31 | If someone can't pay a pending balance at GO but could raise it, they choose: raise the money, or give the property up. | Builder | Section 8.5 says "you may raise money", so it's a choice, not forced. |
| 32 | The pass-the-device screen also appears when one person offers another person a trade. | Builder | The other person needs the device to answer. |
| 33 | The Sim plays half Easy bots and half Random bots by default (`--bots random`, `easy` or `mixed`). | Builder | Random bots test odd situations; Easy bots test sensible play. |

## Phase 8: City Council (2026-10-20)

| # | Decision | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 94 | A City Council session happens at the end of every 4th round (rounds 4, 8, 12...), after that round's timers tick. The session finishes before the next round starts. The last round of a Timed game has no session, because nothing would be left for it to change. | Builder | Section 11.1 says "end of every 4th round" but not what happens in the very last round. |
| 95 | Timed ordinances count down at the end of each round, **before** that round's Regular session. So an ordinance passed at the end of round 4 with a duration of 3 is active for rounds 5, 6 and 7 and ends at the end of round 7. One passed in an Emergency session in the middle of a round counts the rest of that round as one of its rounds. | Builder | Section 11.3 says it lasts "that many rounds" and "ends during the end-of-round checks" but not when counting starts. |
| 96 | Passing an ordinance that is already active (same district, if it has one) restarts its timer instead of adding a second copy. Rent Control and Tourism Boost in the same district both stay active and cancel each other out. | Builder | Stacking two timers of the same card would be confusing, and the rent rules already add percentages. |
| 97 | The Ordinance deck is made once at the start (17 cards, shuffled). Cards that are voted on go to a discard pile, whether they pass or fail. An ordinance that can't be picked, or isn't picked, goes to the **bottom** of the deck. When the draw pile has fewer than 2 cards, the discard pile is shuffled in underneath it. If no card in the whole deck can do anything, the session is called off and the log says so. | Builder | Section 11.2 covers 'neither card can be picked: draw again' but not an empty deck. |
| 98 | "Can be picked" means the ordinance could change something right now: Rezone Up needs a district that is not High-Rise and not frozen (Downzone: not Restricted); Property Tax Hike needs at least one building somewhere; Infrastructure Bond needs a district it can raise; Public Works needs a hidden tile; district ordinances need at least one legal district. If only one of the two drawn cards can be picked, it goes straight to the vote with no question. A district ordinance with only one legal district picks it automatically. | Builder | Section 11.2's example ("Rezone Up on a district that's already High-Rise") is read for every card, so a vote is never wasted on a card that does nothing. |
| 99 | The agenda setter in a Regular session is the lowest **net worth** (the same number as the end-of-game score). A tie goes to the first player after the current player in turn order, so the player whose turn just ended is last in line. In an Emergency session it is the player who caused it. If that player has gone bankrupt, the lowest net worth sets it instead. | Builder | Section 11.1. |
| 100 | Voting is a sealed, simultaneous question for every player who is not bankrupt, in Jail or not. Nothing is taken from anyone's cash until the reveal. You can lobby up to the cash you hold (loans don't count). Abstaining means no lobbying and no Lobbyist card. | Builder | Section 11.3. |
| 101 | The Lobbyist card is ticked on the vote screen instead of being played from your hand beforehand, so nobody can tell how you will vote. It is used up at the reveal and goes to the Opportunity discard pile. It adds its 200 to the side you voted for. The old text on the card ("before the reveal") and the "coming later" label were updated. | Builder | Section 11.3 says the Lobbyist "adds 200 free influence to the player's side" but a card played openly would give away the vote. |
| 102 | The roll is a whole number from 0 up to Yes + No (not including it); the ordinance passes if the roll is below the Yes total. With Yes 500 and No 300 that is exactly a 62.5% chance, and the reveal screen shows the roll as "139 out of 780". The roll comes from the game's seeded random generator, so a replay of the same game gets the same results. | Builder | Section 11.3 step 5 says "a random roll" without saying how. |
| 103 | This replaces #85: the Politician's 200-influence vote now works. Public opinion (100 each side) and the vote value (100) are `rules.json` numbers. | Builder | Phase 8 finishes the Council the Politician needed. |
| 104 | Property Tax Hike and Infrastructure Bond use the normal payment step, so a player who can't cover them is asked to raise money (sell, mortgage, loans) and only goes bankrupt if they still can't pay. Infrastructure Bond: when several districts tie for lowest zoning, the agenda setter picks which goes up (a question after the vote). Frozen districts (Historic Preservation) are skipped when finding the lowest. | Builder | Section 11.4 says the setter breaks ties but not how frozen districts count. |
| 105 | Downzone keeps existing buildings (grandfathering, Section 10.2). Nothing is checked or removed when zoning drops. The zoning rule only stops **new** levels. Historic Preservation lasts for the rest of the game and stops every ordinance from changing that district's zoning. | Builder | Section 10.2 and 11.4. |
| 106 | Building Moratorium blocks building in `BuildingRules.WhyCantBuild` through a new `BuildingBlockedReason` modifier hook. Casino Regulation caps a High/Low bet, a Casino challenge, and the Dice Duel card stake at $200 through a new `MaxCasinoStake` hook. The cap also lowers the "most you can bet" shown to the player. A duel already accepted keeps the stake it was made with. | Builder | The hooks keep the rent, build and Casino code free of any ordinance names (Section 20.6). |
| 107 | The two "coming later" placeholders (`CardNotReadyYet`, `TileNotReadyYet`) were removed. The Emergency Session card and the City Hall tile now start a real Emergency session. City Hall's fee is paid **before** the session. | Builder | Phase 8 replaces the stand-ins. |
| 108 | The Easy bot votes for what helps it and against what hurts it, using simple counts of who owns what in a district, lobbies a little when it feels strongly and has cash to spare, and plays a Lobbyist whenever it takes a side.
